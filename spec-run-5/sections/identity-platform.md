# WP4 — Identity, access control, audit and platform configuration findings

## Findings
| ID | Baseline item | Status | Evidence | Impact | Priority |
|----|---------------|--------|----------|--------|----------|
| I-01 | RG3.1 — a regular administrator may only act on regular users, never on another administrator | implemented | `BanCustomerHandler::__invoke()` rejects targets whose role is `ROLE_ADMIN` or `ROLE_SUPER_ADMIN` (`CannotBanAdminException`); role change and delete are gated by `#[IsGranted('ROLE_SUPER_ADMIN')]` in `AdminCustomerController` (lines 129, 169); the admin list reader restricts visible roles (`AdminCustomerFinder::ADMIN_ROLES`). | None | — |
| I-02 | Permission matrix — administrators may delete regular user accounts | contradictory | `AdminCustomerController::delete()` is guarded by `#[IsGranted('ROLE_SUPER_ADMIN')]` (line 169), while the matrix row "删除普通用户账号" grants the administrator ✔（仅限普通用户）. | A regular administrator cannot close a customer account without super-admin credentials. | must |
| I-03 | RG3.2 — ban requires a written reason and is reversible | implemented | `BanCustomerHandler` rejects an empty reason, stores `ban_reason`, `banned_by`, `banned_at`, and appends `USER_BANNED` to `identity_audit_log`; `UnbanCustomerHandler` clears the fields and appends `USER_UNBANNED`; routes `POST /api/v1/admin/customers/{id}/ban` and `/unban`. | None | — |
| I-04 | RG3.3 — a banned account cannot log in, order or check out | implemented | `CustomerProfileUserProvider::loadUserByIdentifier()` returns `null` when `status === 'banned'`; `security.yaml` requires `ROLE_USER` for `/api/v1/storefront/cart` and for GET/POST `/api/v1/storefront/orders`. Chat is a reserved rule with no surface. | None | — |
| I-05 | RG3.4 — deleting a user with orders anonymizes the profile but preserves financial, snapshot and logistics data | implemented | `DeleteCustomerWithAnonymizationHandler` overwrites `name`/`email`/`phone`, nulls `company`/`mf`, sets `status = 'anonymized'`, deletes the address book, and keeps `ordering_orders` rows intact, including the `customer_name` and `shipping_address` snapshots added in `Version20261017000004` (lines 22–24). "Deactivation" itself does not exist (I-10), so half of the rule has nothing to guard. | The rule holds for deletion and demotion in normal use, but not under concurrency, and cannot be enforced for deactivation. | should |
| I-09 | RG4.1 — only a logged-in, non-banned user may use the cart and checkout | implemented | `security.yaml` access control: `^/api/v1/storefront/cart` and GET/POST `^/api/v1/storefront/orders` require `ROLE_USER`; banned users cannot authenticate (I-04). `^/api/v1/storefront/cart/quote` is public by design for anonymous price display, not cart use. | None | — |

| I-11 | RC13 — deactivating an administrator revokes their live credentials and rejects in-flight writes | absent | No deactivation transition exists (I-10), so no revocation point exists. ## Notes
- Identity carries more capability than the BRS asks for. Wishlist, product reviews, newsletter subscription, quote requests, contact messages, FAQ and Google OAuth are implemented and are **extras**, listed once in `gap-analysis.md` (R10), not gaps.
- Address editing exists through the upsert form of `POST /addresses`; the gap is the default-address flag, not create/edit/delete.
- The administrator lifecycle gap (I-10) is the root cause of the permission contradictions: `ROLE_ADMIN` exists in the hierarchy and in `access_control`, and most admin controllers already accept it, but no account can hold it.
- I-17 duplicates C-06/C-14 by design; the consolidated report counts it once.
